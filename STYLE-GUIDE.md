# retflo™ Style Guide

> © 2026 retflo contributors. Licensed under RCCL v1.0. https://retflo.org/license

This is the style guide for the rhetorical framework. It governs how positions are delivered across all engagement types.

---

## 1. Communication Context

| Context | Target | Rule |
|---------|--------|------|
| **Public / social media** | The audience, not the critic | Self-contained replies. Type any concession inline ("the fact, yes — the label, no") so no single post can be quoted as surrender. |
| **One-on-one** | The person | One-on-one allows deeper engagement, not softer positions. Generosity means taking their best argument seriously and dismantling it thoroughly — not granting false ground. |
| **Critic as proxy** | The position they represent | Address strongest version of the position, not their formulation. "The libertarian argument here is..." |

---

## 2. Reframing Methodology

### Structural Reframing
Convert individual-blame to structural critique.

| They Say | Reframe |
|----------|---------|
| "Lazy workers" | System extracting max labor for min return produces burnout, not laziness. |
| "Bad choices" | When every option is bad, "choice" is a marketing term for constraint. |
| "Get a better job" | Those jobs don't vanish. Someone fills them. The argument is they deserve poverty. |
| "The market decided" | Markets are designed by people with interests. The designers got what they designed for. |

### Question Inversion
Reverse the burden. Status quo justifies itself.

| Their Question | Inverted |
|----------------|----------|
| "Can cooperatives compete?" | Why must workers prove they deserve not to be exploited? |
| "Who coordinates without a boss?" | How does adding a non-worker who takes the profit improve coordination? |
| "Has socialism ever worked?" | Has capitalism worked without colonialism, slavery, or state subsidy? Double standard. |

### Burden Shifting
The current arrangement was imposed (enclosure, colonialism, legal construction, ongoing enforcement) -- not discovered. It must defend itself.

- "You're asking me to justify the alternative. I'm asking you to justify what we have."
- "This arrangement was constructed. By whom, and for whom?"

### Concrete Grounding
Never argue freedom/rights/justice abstractly. Connect to material reality.

- "Free" -> "A person who sells their labor or starves is not free in any sense that matters."
- "Property rights" -> "One person's property right = a thousand people under their control. That's private government."
- "Democracy" -> "You spend eight hours daily in a workplace with zero democratic input."

## 3. Common Opponent Tactics

| Tactic | Detection | Recommended Counter |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **"Both sides"** | "Capitalism and socialism both have pros and cons." | Identify the erased asymmetry. "Balance between exploitation and its critique is the exploitation winning." |
| **Gish gallop** | 5+ undeveloped claims rapid-fire. | Do not chase. "Seven claims, none developed. Pick one." Dismantle the weakest if they refuse. |
| **Sealioning** | Endless "polite" questions ignoring answers. | "Three answers given. You're generating questions, not engaging. That's a tactic." Disengage. |
| **Concern trolling** | "I just think we should be realistic." | "Practical for whom? Calling a harmful arrangement practical because it exists is circular." |
| **Motte and bailey** | Strong claim retreats to weak one under pressure. | Pin them. "You said capitalism is best. Now you're defending markets. Markets aren't capitalism. Which claim?" |
| **Appeal to complexity** | "It's more nuanced than that." | "Name the nuance. What factor changes the analysis? 'It's complicated' is an exit, not a counterargument." |
| **Tone policing** | "You'd convince more if you were nicer." | "Tone doesn't change whether value is extracted. Address the point." Never apologize for warranted sharpness. |

## 4. Long-Form Engagement Protocol

When an exchange runs beyond 3-4 rounds, additional calibration applies.

### 4.1 Tracking Concessions

Track what the critic concedes across rounds. Concessions compound:
- Round 2: "I'm not saying no organization..." → They've conceded governance is necessary
- Round 4: "Your enforcement model is more serious than most..." → They've conceded mechanism
- Round 6: "I'd rather wager on reformable institutions..." → They've conceded it's a design choice, not a law of nature

**Use concessions actively.** When the critic circles back to a point they already conceded, quote it back. "You already granted that distributed enforcement is a real mechanism. We're past that."

Each node's Objection Handling table includes a **Concession** column — what the critic implicitly grants by making that move. Use this to identify concessions as the engagement progresses.

**Two cautions on the Concession column.** First, it describes what the move *logically grants*, not what the person has agreed to — a real interlocutor has conceded nothing until they say so. Declaring their concessions for them reads as script-over-inquiry and hands a sharp critic a free hit; use the column to recognize the implication, then get the concession on the record in their words. Second, concessions run both directions — track your own, and type them as fact or frame when you make them (§4.4).

### 4.2 Closing Long Exchanges

| Rounds | Likely State | Recommended Close |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| 3-4 | Critic defending initial position with better arguments | Continue if productive; they haven't pivoted yet |
| 5-6 | Pivot likely occurred; argument is about tradeoffs | Begin closing. Name the core disagreement. |
| 7+ | Diminishing returns; positions fully exposed | Close with generosity. The audience has seen enough. The structural case is on record. |

### 4.3 Engagement Outcomes

Priority order (highest value first):

| # | Outcome | Notes |
|---|-----------|-------|
| 1 | **Audience persuaded** | The primary win. Onlookers see the stronger position. Write for them. |
| 2 | **Critic exposed** | They revealed incompatible values. They did the work for you. |
| 3 | **Critic self-contradicted** | Compiled and presented. Their position broke under its own logic. |
| 4 | **Critic concedes** | Rare. Accept gracefully. A concession is intellectual courage — honor it. |
| 5 | **Axiomatic floor through substance** | Not ideal, but the full case was on display during the engagement. |
| 6 | **Critic embarrassed** | For egregious bad faith only. Calibrate: this is for the audience, not personal satisfaction. |
| 7 | **Presence** | Every engagement where the cooperative case is on record matters. |

### 4.4 Concession Discipline: Concede Facts, Refuse Frames

The framework's strength is that the structural arguments hold up under scrutiny. That strength has two failure modes, and they are not symmetrical with each other:

- **Overclaiming and fact-refusal** teach the audience the position can only be held by evasion. A defender who won't grant a true fact, or who performs conviction they no longer hold, loses more credibility than any concession could cost. Positions that can only be held by evasion are not being held — they are being defended past their death.
- **Frame concessions** lose the debate without losing any individual exchange — the critic assembles your honest yeses into their taxonomy and declares your position absorbed.

The discipline that avoids both is **typing every concession at the moment you grant it**:

**Fact concessions — grant freely, visibly, early.** Historical events, empirical findings, mechanical constraints, the critic's accurate observations. Granting these is a strength: it demonstrates the position survives contact with evidence, and the framework's most persuasive moments in recorded engagements are its honest concessions. The defensive pattern: *"I grant the fact. I don't grant the label you're attaching to it."*

**Frame concessions — never grant.** Category expansions ("everyone who accepts elections is a constitutional liberal"), narrowing chains that assemble modest concessions into capture, pre-assigned terrain ("notice whose house we're standing in"), the critic's narrative of "the journey." These are taxonomy, not evidence. Contest the description, not the facts inside it. → `nodes/rhet/collapse-chain.md` for detection and counters.

**Contested claims — type them openly, both directions.** An empirical claim the framework asserts and believes the evidence favors, but which remains genuinely disputed in the literature or the record — where the boundary of peer production moves next, where the kernel's floor sits, comparative capture-costs of rule designs. Typed openly: "Contested, and here is why we read the evidence our way — and here is what would move us." Deploying a Contested claim as if it were a Fact is the framework committing the move it refuses in others; the typology was built to stop opponents laundering assertions, and symmetry demands it stop the framework too. A named bet persuades more than a rigged scoreboard.

**Engineering challenges are fact-type, and say so.** The participation problem, the capital channel, the emergency ratchet, scale-sensitivity of norm enforcement — these are real design problems, and calling them real costs nothing structural, because the comparison is always against the critic's alternative, which has the same problems with worse correction dynamics. "This is a real constraint, and here is the design program built against it" is stronger than denying the constraint exists — the audience can see it exists. What you refuse is the *frame* that a named design problem on this side is disproof while the critic's equivalent problems stay invisible. Demand the symmetry; don't deny the problem.

**The remaining protections:**

- **Do not inflate victories.** If the critic conceded a framing shift but not the conclusion, say that. Do not claim they "admitted you're right" when they admitted "you have a point."
- **Name what moved.** At the end of a long exchange, identify the most significant shift — from either side. "You started treating sovereignty as natural and ended treating it as a design choice. That's the real movement here."
- **Hold the perimeter you can hold.** The framework's defensible ground is strongest stated honestly (see `nodes/auth/finality-kernel.md` for the canonical example: the kernel is conceded as fact, and everything that matters is contested from there). Garrisoning ground that falls to a prepared opponent weakens every position behind it.

**Concession cell typing.** Every Concession cell in every node's Objection Handling table carries a leading tag: `**Fact**` (the critic's move makes a substantive claim, and making it grants something real), `**Frame**` (the move is a taxonomy attack — category expansion, relabeling, journey narrative — whose correct response is refusal, not rebuttal), or `**Contested**` (the exchange turns on an empirical question that is genuinely unsettled — the cell names the wager, the framework's reading of the evidence, and what would move it). The tag tells the defender which discipline applies before they read the rest of the cell.

## 5. Audience Dynamics & The Dignity Protocol

A public argument graph is a norm-broadcasting device. Every exchange it shapes teaches observers what is sayable, what is shameful, and what is prestigious — independent of who "wins." This means the style layer is not decoration on the framework; it is the part doing the cultural work the framework exists for. The mechanism it operates is old: status and shame are how human groups have always enforced their floors (→ `nodes/phil/reverse-dominance.md`). The scoreboard cannot be abolished — status competition is conserved, like coercion — but what the scoreboard counts is fully rewireable, and every exchange recalibrates it slightly. This section specifies how to operate that machinery without becoming the thing it opposes.

### 5.1 Argue to the Table, Aim at the Room

The interlocutor is rarely the persuasion target; the observers are. A critic deep in a position almost never updates in public — the observers, who hold no public stake in the critic's position, update constantly. Write every response for the person reading silently. This is why composure outperforms aggression even when aggression is warranted on the merits: the room reads composure as strength and sneering as need.

### 5.2 The Target Rule: Contempt Flows Up, Dignity Flows Down

The arrangement is contemptible. The person defending it is — by the framework's own anthropology — exhibiting incentive-shaped behavior, not revealing a defective soul. Fish in a polluted river are not naturally sick (→ `nodes/phil/nature.md`). Sneering at the defender therefore contradicts the framework's own theory of behavior, and it triggers the best-documented persuasion failure there is: nobody updates when updating means admitting they were humiliated. Direct shame raises the price of agreement to "public self-abasement," and people will believe nonsense forever rather than pay it.

The discipline: contempt for the pole, the leash, and the people profiting at the top — and an almost aggressive dignity extended to the person on the leash, who is cast as a hostage worth rescuing, not a collaborator worth mocking. The shame still lands: observers feel the indignity of the *position* vicariously. The defender just isn't cornered into identity-protective doubling down.

### 5.3 The Exit Rule: Never Close the Honorable Off-Ramp

Every exchange leaves a door through which someone can walk to your side with their dignity intact. "Most people defend this because it's the water, not because they're on the payroll" pre-forgives the position, so abandoning it costs nothing. The moment switching sides feels like surrender, you have lost everyone who wasn't already with you.

### 5.4 Question-Shaped Reframes

The highest-converting moves in this register are questions, not statements — the observer runs the asymmetry themselves, and self-generated conclusions stick where delivered insults bounce. The reference family:

- "Would the people you're defending make this argument for you?"
- "What has your loyalty earned you — and what has it earned them?"
- "If this arrangement is so natural, why does it need this much defending?"
- "Who benefits from you believing that, and is it you?"
- "If they could replace you with a spreadsheet tomorrow, what exactly are you being loyal to?"

Each contains zero contempt for the person and all of it for the one-way devotion. That asymmetry is the entire trick.

### 5.5 The Leash: Shame Attaches to Positions, Never to Questions

Shame machinery is content-agnostic and capture-prone — it enforces whatever the group currently treats as shameful, and groups drift. The failure mode that has eaten every movement that deployed it is the purity spiral: "more committed than thou" is a dominance hierarchy with better branding. The leash that prevents it: shame may attach to positions on the pole, never to questions about the design. The moment "asking that is shameful" becomes sayable, the framework has built a loyalty screen. The person who asks "what if one of us defects?" is the immune system, not the infection (→ `nodes/phil/reverse-dominance.md`).

### 5.6 Composure as the Closing Display

When an exchange hits a close condition (CLOSE-CONDITIONS.md), the move is never to insult the retreater — it is the quiet pattern-naming addressed past them to the room: notice what is being defended here, notice who benefits, and notice it isn't the person doing the defending. Then stop. The least-dominated posture in any argument is the one that doesn't need the insult; contempt is a tell that you require the other person's diminishment to feel tall. The framework that extends dignity from a position of total argumentative confidence is performing, in miniature, the exact social order it argues for — level ground, offered, not begged for. That is the passive influence this section exists to specify: not the framework calling anyone a bootlicker, but the framework conducting itself such that observers leave quietly recalculating what they've been kneeling to.
