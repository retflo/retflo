# The Dignity Asymmetry: Anti-Domination Affect, Routed to the Wrong Address

## Position

The most powerful political feeling in the vernacular is not solidarity or fairness. It is the refusal to be anyone's subject: *don't tread on me, be nobody's bitch, I kneel to no one.* That affect — self-ownership, dignity, anti-domination — is the framework's own emotional core. And it is currently harvested, almost without contest, by movements that recruit people *into* hierarchy.

### The Routing Error

The Gadsden-flag instinct is anti-domination affect aimed at the state's most visible agents — the meter maid, the tax form, the permit office. Meanwhile the median person's actual daily experience of being commanded comes from somewhere else entirely: the boss sets their hours, gates their healthcare, times their bathroom breaks, monitors their keystrokes, and can end their livelihood in a ten-minute meeting. The boss treads on the median person a thousand times for every time a bureaucrat does. The feeling is correct; the address is wrong — and nobody contests the routing, so the right keeps the vocabulary and uses it to shame people *up* the pole: in that lexicon the non-submissive man is the one climbing, grinding for the approval of the very hierarchy that owns him.

### The Scoreboard Is Conserved

Status competition cannot be abolished — like the coercion on the island, it is conserved. What confers status is fully rewireable: societies have run prestige gradients on generosity, restraint, and refusal before, and the forager record is a prestige gradient run on *suppressing* dominance (→ `phil/reverse-dominance.md`). The left already operates one working piece of this machinery — "bootlicker" makes deference shameful, and nobody argues their way out of it; they feel it. The full move is squeezing the pole from both ends: contempt for lording it, contempt for kneeling, until standing level is the only position that doesn't cost face. The genuinely high-status act — the one that should earn the room — is declining to climb *and* declining to kneel, in public, where it costs something.

### The Discipline That Keeps This From Becoming an Insult Engine

The mechanism is content-agnostic and capture-prone — shame engines drift, and "more radical than thou" is a dominance hierarchy with better branding. Two rules keep it aimed (full protocol: STYLE-GUIDE §5):

1. **Contempt flows up, dignity flows down.** The arrangement is contemptible; the person defending it is exhibiting incentive-shaped behavior — the framework's own anthropology (fish in a polluted river, → `phil/nature.md`). Sneer at the defender and you contradict your own theory of behavior while triggering the best-documented persuasion failure there is: nobody updates when updating means admitting humiliation. The defender is a hostage worth rescuing, not a collaborator worth mocking. The shame still lands — observers feel the indignity of the position vicariously — while the door stays open.
2. **Shame attaches to positions, never to questions.** The moment "asking that is shameful" becomes sayable, the machinery has become a loyalty screen — the failure mode that has eaten every movement that deployed it.

### The Question-Shaped Version

The highest-converting form of this entire move is a question, not an insult: **"Would the people you're defending make this argument for you?"** It is unanswerable in their favor, it makes the observer run the loyalty asymmetry themselves — and self-generated conclusions stick where delivered insults bounce — and it contains zero contempt for the person, all of it for the one-way devotion.

## Objection Handling

| Move | Response | Concession |
|---|---|---|
| "Government is the real tyranny — don't tread on me" | Count the treadings. Who set your schedule today, gated your healthcare, monitored your output, and could end your income this afternoon? The instinct is exactly right: be nobody's subject. Now follow it to the authority you actually live under eight hours a day. → `auth/state-class-instrument.md` | **Fact** — grants that being dominated is intolerable, the framework's core premise; the remaining dispute is only over which dominations the critic has been taught to see |
| "At least I can quit my job — I can't quit the government" | The state can conscript and cage; that asymmetry is real and granted. But measure the employer's leverage honestly: it equals the thinness of your outside options — and for most people, "quit" means losing healthcare, housing security, and next month's rent. An exit that costs ruin is a door painted on a wall. And notice the remedy you've implied: thicker exit, less dependence — which is a demand against concentrated ownership, not a defense of it. → `econ/exit-and-class.md` | **Fact** — grants that exit is the test of whether power is tolerable, making exit's actual thinness the critic's problem to explain |
| "You're just trying to shame people into agreeing" | The shame attaches to arrangements, never to the people inside them; the framework's own anthropology says the defender is exhibiting incentive-shaped behavior, not a defective soul. What's being named is an asymmetry: one-way loyalty toward people who would not return it. If naming that lands as shame, the shame was already in the arrangement. | **Frame** — attempts to recast structural description as personal attack; refuse the recast, restate the target rule, leave the off-ramp open |
| "Status games are human nature — your level-ground utopia ignores that" | Conceded: the scoreboard is conserved. What it counts is not. Forager bands ran prestige on dominance-suppression for tens of millennia; honor cultures ran it on generosity and restraint. The proposal was never abolishing status — it's rewiring what earns it, which the record shows is fully possible. → `phil/reverse-dominance.md` | **Fact** — grants that status mechanics are real and powerful, which makes the design question (what the scoreboard counts) the live one, and that question has historical answers |
| "Calling people cucks and bootlickers — that's your movement's discourse?" | The vernacular exists and is currently owned by movements that use it to shame people into hierarchy; pretending the register doesn't exist cedes it. The framework's actual protocol runs the opposite of an insult engine: contempt for the pole, dignity for the person on it, and the question form — "would they make this argument for you?" — which contains no insult at all. | **Frame** — attempts to indict the framework by its crudest possible form; refuse the substitution, the protocol on record is the opposite of the caricature |
